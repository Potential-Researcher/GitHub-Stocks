Three Hyundais have a 360 camera under $35K

Hyundai calls it the Surround View Monitor, and it is a top-trim-only feature — there is no package that adds it to a cheaper car. That single fact decides the whole search.

The short answer

Only the Elantra Limited, Elantra Hybrid Limited, and Kona Limited pair a factory 360-degree camera with a sticker under $35K. The Elantra Limited is the clear value pick at $27,175 — it leaves roughly $6,500 of room under the ceiling for tax, insurance, and winter tires. The Kona Limited only clears $35K on a front-wheel-drive car, and only if the ceiling means MSRP rather than what you actually hand the dealer.

The trim trap

Several 2026 Hyundais land comfortably under $35K and still have no 360 camera — the Elantra N Line at $29,400, the Elantra SEL Sport Premium at $25,075, the Tucson SEL Premium at $33,550, the Santa Cruz SEL at $33,000. Spending more money on the wrong model gets you nothing here. Hyundai does not sell the Surround View Monitor as a standalone option or a package, so the rule is simply: buy the cheapest Hyundai whose Limited trim fits the budget. Today that is the Elantra.

Watch the language at the dealership too. A "multi-view rear camera" or a wide-angle backup camera is not the same system. The window sticker will spell out Surround View Monitor by name if the car has it.

The plan

In order — each step narrows the search before the next one costs anyone a Saturday.

Decide whether $35K means sticker or out-the-door

Tax, title, and registration typically add 8–10%. If $35K is the amount actually leaving her bank account, the working MSRP target is closer to $31K — which keeps both Elantra Limiteds in and pushes the Kona Limited out. Settle this first; it decides the shortlist.

Search inventory by trim, not by model

On the Hyundai site or any dealer's inventory page, filter to Limited and nothing else. Most listings in the price range will be SEL trims that look nearly identical in photos and lack the camera entirely.

Confirm the camera on the window sticker

Before any test drive, ask the dealer to send a photo of the Monroney sticker for that specific VIN. Look for the "Surround View Monitor" line. This takes two minutes and removes the single most likely way to buy the wrong car.

Get her an insurance quote before signing anything

A new driver's premium can vary by a thousand dollars a year between these cars — the Kona Limited's 190-hp turbo rates differently from the Elantra's 147-hp four. Run quotes on the exact trims while she is still deciding, not after.

Ask three dealers for an itemized out-the-door quote

Same trim, same options, in writing. Compare the bottom lines, not the monthly payments. Dealer add-ons — paint protection, nitrogen-filled tires, VIN etching, fabric sealant — are negotiable and usually removable; ask for them to come off.

Check what Hyundai is offering that month

Hyundai routinely runs a recent-graduate rebate, first-responder and military programs, and subsidized financing. These stack with the negotiated price. Also worth knowing: every new Hyundai carries a 10-year/100,000-mile powertrain warranty, which matters more than usual on a first car.

What to actually test on the drive

Written for a new driver — these are the things that are hard to judge from a spec sheet.

Park it using only the 360 view. Find the tightest spot in the lot and pull in without turning around.
Check the screen at dusk or in rain. Camera quality varies; a grainy night image undoes the whole point.
Signal on the highway. Confirm the blind-spot camera feed appears in the gauge cluster and that she finds it useful, not distracting.
Listen to the parking sensors. Make sure the beeping is legible rather than panic-inducing.
Sit in the driver's seat and look out. Sedan vs. small SUV changes the seating height and over-the-shoulder view more than the camera does.
On the Kona, try Remote Smart Parking Assist. If it clicks for her, it may justify the extra money on its own.
